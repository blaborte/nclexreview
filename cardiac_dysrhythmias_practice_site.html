Core concepts

Cardiac dysrhythmias are abnormal rhythms originating from the heart's electrical conduction system that impair its ability to pump blood effectively. Ineffective pumping compromises perfusion — blood flow to organs — leading to dysfunction or systemic failure.

Ventricular dysrhythmias are significantly more dangerous than atrial dysrhythmias because the left ventricle supplies oxygenated blood to vital organs.

ECG basics & measurement standards

P waveAtrial depolarization → atrial systole/contraction
QRS complexVentricular depolarization → ventricular systole/contraction
T waveVentricular repolarization → ventricular diastole, relaxation & passive filling
Grid measureTime / Voltage
1 small square (1 mm)0.04 sec / 0.1 mV
1 large square (5 mm)0.20 sec / 0.5 mV
5 large squares (25 mm)1.00 second
Standard calibration10 mm = 1.0 mV

Steps for rhythm interpretation: rate → rhythm regularity → P waves (presence, uniformity, ratio) → PR interval (0.12–0.20 sec) → QRS duration (narrow: 0.04–0.12 sec) → QT interval.

Six-second method: count R waves in a 6-second strip, multiply by 10, for average bpm.

Sinus bradycardia & A-fib/flutter — detailed management

Sinus bradycardia causes: athletic conditioning, sleep, hypothyroidism, vagal response (vomiting, suctioning, straining), beta-blockers/CCBs, increased ICP, MI.

Symptoms: shortness of breath, chest pain, dizziness, fatigue, hypotension, altered mental status. Management: withhold causal meds; if symptomatic, O2 + atropine 1 mg IV; prepare TCP if refractory.

A-Fib "ABCDE" mnemonic

Anticoagulants (prevent embolic events) · Beta-blockers (slow AV node conduction) · Calcium channel blockers (verapamil, diltiazem) · Digoxin (rate control in HF) · Electrocardioversion (resets sinus rhythm)

Complications: loss of atrial kick decreases cardiac output; blood stagnation in atria → thrombus formation → DVT, PE, ischemic stroke risk.

Heart blocks — "the heart block poem"

1st Degree: "If the R is far from P, then you have a First Degree." PR interval >0.20 sec; usually asymptomatic.
2nd Degree Type I (Wenckebach): "Longer, longer, longer, drop! Now you have a Wenckebach." Progressive PR prolongation until a QRS is dropped.
2nd Degree Type II (Mobitz II): "If some Ps don't get through, then you have a Mobitz II." Fixed PR intervals with intermittent dropped QRS — requires pacing.
3rd Degree (Complete): "If Ps and Qs don't agree, then you have a Third Degree." Atria & ventricles beat independently — medical emergency, temporary TCP + permanent pacemaker.

Pacemakers & implantable devices

AICDContinuously monitors for VT/VF; automatically delivers internal countershocks (25–30 J).
Permanent/demand pacemakerDelivers an electrical pulse to capture the myocardium when intrinsic rate drops below threshold.

Complications to monitor: infection, pneumothorax, hemothorax, hematoma, catheter displacement/ventricular ectopy.

Acute coronary syndrome & MI

Pathophysiology: atherosclerotic plaque rupture → thrombus formation → myocardium deprived of blood/O2. Physical ischemia changes within 6 hr; infarct tissue turns gray with yellow streaks by 48 hr.

Classic manifestations: substernal chest pain radiating to jaw, neck, left arm, or back; dyspnea, diaphoresis, nausea/vomiting, pallor, dizziness, anxiety.

Atypical (women/diabetics/elderly): epigastric distress/indigestion, unexplained extreme fatigue, shortness of breath, upper back discomfort.

MI complications — "DARTH VADER"

Death · Arrhythmias · Rupture (papillary muscle, septum, free wall) · Tamponade · Heart failure · Valve disease · Aneurysm (ventricular) · Dressler's syndrome · Embolism (mural thrombus) · Recurrence/mitral Regurgitation

Hospital protocol for acute chest pain: assess pain/VS/ECG → O2 + Semi-Fowler's → 1 SL NTG tablet → reassess pain/BP in 5 min → repeat up to 3 doses total (5 min apart) if BP stable → contact PHCP if unresolved after 3rd dose.

Heart failure & acute pulmonary edema

Left-sided (pulmonary)Right-sided (systemic)
Pulmonary congestion, dyspnea/orthopnea, tachypnea, dry hacking cough, frothy sputumJVD, peripheral edema, ascites & hepatomegaly, weight gain/anorexia

Acute pulmonary edema — emergency steps: High-Fowler's position immediately → high-flow O2 → assess lung sounds → IV access → prepare furosemide + IV morphine sulfate (reduces preload/anxiety) → Foley catheter for output → prepare for intubation if respiratory failure.

Cardiovascular pharmacology summary

ThrombolyticsAlteplase, tenecteplase. Dissolve arterial clots. Check absolute contraindications (active bleeding, recent surgery, head trauma). Neuro checks q15–30min. Antidote: aminocaproic acid (Amicar).
AntiplateletsAspirin, clopidogrel. Prevent platelet aggregation. Watch for occult GI bleed, black tarry stools, bruising, hematuria.
NitratesNitroglycerin, isosorbide. Vasodilation ↓ preload & O2 demand. Side effects: hypotension, throbbing headache.
Beta-blockersAtenolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol. ↓ HR, BP, workload. Hold if HR <60 bpm or SBP <90 mmHg.
AnticholinergicAtropine. Increases SA node firing to treat symptomatic sinus bradycardia.
