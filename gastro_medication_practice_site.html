Antiulcer drug classifications

Antacids · H2 receptor antagonists · antibacterials · proton pump inhibitors · sucralfate · misoprostol

AntacidsNeutralize gastric acid, raise pH, inactivate pepsin. Ca²⁺ (Tums), Na (Alka-Seltzer), Mg (Milk of Magnesia), Al (Amphogel).
H2 receptor antagonistsSuppress gastric acid secretion. "-tidine" — cimetidine, ranitidine, famotidine, nizatidine.
AntibacterialsTreat H. pylori infection. Amoxicillin, clarithromycin, tetracycline, metronidazole.
Proton pump inhibitorsInhibit the proton pump, decrease acid secretion. "-prazole" — omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, rabeprazole.
Sucralfate (Carafate)Protective barrier against acid/pepsin — does NOT decrease acid secretion. Give 30 min apart from other meds.
Misoprostol (Cytotec)Replaces prostaglandins, protects gastric mucosa. Contraindicated in pregnancy/lactation — teach contraception.

Priority nursing actions: assess bowel function, renal function, abdominal pain, fluid balance. Administer after meals; chew tablets and swallow with water; shake liquid preparations.

Antiemetics — act on the chemoreceptor trigger zone (CTZ) & vestibular system

PhenothiazinesBlock CTZ receptors. Prochlorperazine (Compazine). Adverse: sedation, EPS, anticholinergic symptoms.
Serotonin (5-HT3) antagonistsBlock CTZ receptors. "-setron" — ondansetron, dolasetron. Adverse: headache, diarrhea, dizziness.
ProkineticsIncrease GI motility, relax pyloric sphincter. Metoclopramide (Reglan). Adverse: EPS, nausea, diarrhea — watch for tardive dyskinesia with long-term use.
AntihistaminesBlock CTZ & vomiting center. Meclizine (Antivert), dimenhydrinate (Dramamine). Contraindicated in glaucoma.
TrimethobenzamideBlocks CTZ & vomiting center. Tigan. Same profile as antihistamines — sedation, anticholinergic effects, glaucoma contraindication.

Case study — tardive dyskinesia

Older adult with chronic kidney disease, hypertension, liver disease, GERD, and alcohol abuse presents with new uncontrolled movements of the hands and face (tongue movements, cheek puffing) after 4 months of metoclopramide for GERD. The nurse is most concerned about tardive dyskinesia — reduced renal clearance from kidney disease increases drug exposure and EPS risk.

Genitourinary medications

Nitrofurantoin (Macrodantin/Macrobid)Bacteriostatic/bactericidal for lower UTIs. Precaution: pregnancy, neonates, renal/liver disease, antacid interaction. Adverse: peripheral neuropathy, anemia/thrombocytopenia/leukopenia. Give with food; complete full course; increase fluids.
Phenazopyridine (Pyridium)Urinary analgesic — decreases burning, frequency, urgency. Expected: reddish-orange urine, sclera discoloration (contact lens caution). Take after meals with full glass of water.
AnticholinergicsOxybutynin, darifenacin, solifenacin, tolterodine, trospium — for overactive bladder/urge incontinence. Contraindicated: narrow angle glaucoma, urinary retention, intestinal obstruction. Adverse: dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision.

Suffix quick reference

"-tidine"H2 receptor antagonist — cimetidine, famotidine, nizatidine.
"-prazole"Proton pump inhibitor — omeprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole.
"-setron"5-HT3 (serotonin) antagonist antiemetic — ondansetron, dolasetron, granisetron, palonosetron.

Gastric acid suppressing agents (PPIs & H2RAs): avoid alcohol and NSAIDs; monitor for epigastric/abdominal pain, occult blood, confusion (H2RAs); watch for C. diff diarrhea and vitamin B12 deficiency with long-term use.
